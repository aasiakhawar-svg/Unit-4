1The Locked Lab5 min · Q1, Q2

Night. Detective Carbon finds a note taped to the lab door: a drawing of C with four CH3-type arms.

Carbon: It shows how atoms are arranged, but the C–H bonds are not all drawn. What kind of formula is this?
Case file: Q1
D, structural formula. It shows the groups on each carbon but not every bond (a displayed formula would draw them all).
Assistant: The suspects all belong to one family, a homologous series. What must be true of them?
Case file: Q2
C, they contain the same functional group. Members differ by CH2 (not CH3), have similar chemical properties and gradually changing physical properties.
2The Name Tag Mix-up5 min · Q3

A chlorinated suspect refuses to give its name. Its chain: ClCH2–CHCl–CH2–CH3

Carbon: Four carbons in a row, no double bond, two chlorines on the first two carbons.
Case file: Q3
B, 1,2-dichlorobutane. "-ane" because there are only single bonds (so not "-ene"), and the Cl atoms sit on carbons 1 and 2.
3Fuel Market Heist6 min · Q4, Q5

Someone stole the "carbon neutral" sign from the fuel stalls: ethanol, natural gas, petrol, wood.

Case file: Q4
D, wood. Trees absorb as much CO2 while growing as is released on burning. Ethanol from biomass can also be argued, but wood is the intended answer here.
Carbon: Then a halogen sneaks in and swaps an H. Which equation is the swap (substitution)?
Case file: Q5
C: C2H6 + F2 → C2H5F + HF. One H is replaced by F. A is hydration, B is addition, D is dehydrogenation.
4Radical Chain Chase6 min · Q6

Under UV light, chlorine molecules split into radicals and race through the city.

Carbon: The chase ends when two radicals meet and no new radical is made. Which step is that?
Case file: Q6
A: •CH2Cl + Cl• → CH2Cl2. Two radicals combine into a molecule: termination. C and D are propagation, and B is not a real step in this reaction.
5The Boiling Point Ladder8 min · Q7 a-b

Detective Carbon climbs a ladder of alkanes, and the temperature rises with every rung.

AlkaneFormulab.p. / K
heptaneC7H16372
octane?399
nonaneC9H20?
decaneC10H22447
(a) Formula of octane
C8H18 (CnH2n+2).
(b)(i) Explain the trend
Longer chains have more electrons and a larger surface area, so stronger London (induced dipole–dipole) forces between molecules. More energy is needed to overcome them, so the boiling temperature rises.
(b)(ii) Predict nonane
About 423 K, roughly halfway between octane (399) and decane (447). Anything near 420–425 K is sensible.
6The Cracking Machine7 min · Q7 c

A giant machine at the refinery smashes long chains into shorter, more useful ones.

(c)(i) Crack decane into octane + ethene
C10H22 → C8H18 + C2H4
(c)(ii) Ethene to ethanol
C2H4 + H2O ⇄ C2H5OH
Conditions: steam, about 300 °C, about 60–70 atm, phosphoric acid catalyst (on a silica support).
7The Reforming Workshop8 min · Q7 d

Straight chains walk in, and bent, branched or ringed molecules walk out.

(d)(i) Decane → 2,3-dimethyloctane
Skeletal formulae: a 10-carbon zigzag on the left, an arrow, then an 8-carbon zigzag with a methyl branch on both carbon 2 and carbon 3. The molecular formula stays C10H22: it is only rearranged.
(d)(ii) Heptane → methylcyclohexane
C7H16 → C7H14 + H2
Draw a 7-carbon zigzag, then a six-carbon ring with one CH3 branch, plus H2. (Don't forget the hydrogen!)
(d)(iii) Why reform for petrol?
Branched and cyclic alkanes burn more smoothly, which means a higher octane rating and less engine knocking (auto-ignition) than straight chains.
8Who Is Suspect Y?10 min · Q8

A hydrocarbon, 85.7% carbon by mass, hides in the shadows. Mr = 56.

(a)(i) Empirical formula
%H = 14.3.
C: 85.7 ÷ 12 = 7.14 mol. H: 14.3 ÷ 1 = 14.3 mol.
Ratio C:H = 1 : 2, so the empirical formula is CH2.
(a)(ii) Molecular formula
Mr of CH2 = 14. 56 ÷ 14 = 4, so the formula is (CH2)4 = C4H8.
(b) The six isomers (4 unsaturated, 2 saturated)
Unsaturated (contain C=C):
1. but-1-ene: CH2=CH–CH2–CH3
2. cis-but-2-ene: CH3 groups on the same side of C=C
3. trans-but-2-ene: CH3 groups on opposite sides
4. 2-methylpropene: CH2=C(CH3)–CH3
Saturated (rings):
5. cyclobutane: a ring of 4 C atoms
6. methylcyclopropane: a 3-C ring with a CH3
When drawing displayed formulae, show every C–H bond. The question says "five" but lists six isomers, so draw all six to be safe.
9The Final Line-up8 min · Q9

Four suspects stand in a line: A (4 C), B (5 C), C (6 C, bent) and D (6 C, bent differently).

(a) Homologous series
Alkanes. They contain only C–C and C–H single bonds (saturated), with general formula CnH2n+2.
(b) Which formulae show only one compound?
C and D are the same compound: both are unbranched hexane, just drawn with the chain bent in different places. Bends in a drawing don't change the order the atoms are joined. A (butane) and B (pentane) are different compounds from each other and from hexane.
(c) Isomers of B (pentane)
2-methylbutane: CH3–CH(CH3)–CH2–CH3
2,2-dimethylpropane: CH3–C(CH3)2–CH3

BThe Combustion Chamber6 min · bonus

A furnace roars. One door has plenty of oxygen, the other very little.

1. Write the equation for complete combustion of propane.
2. Write an equation for incomplete combustion of methane to give CO.
3. Why is CO dangerous?
4. 2.2 g of propane burns completely. What volume of CO2 forms at RTP (24 dm3 mol-1)?
Reveal
1 C3H8 + 5O2 → 3CO2 + 4H2O
2 2CH4 + 3O2 → 2CO + 4H2O
3 CO binds to haemoglobin, stopping blood carrying oxygen. It is colourless and odourless.
4 Mr propane = 44, so 2.2 g = 0.05 mol. CO2 = 0.15 mol, and 0.15 × 24 = 3.6 dm3.
CThe Radical Trial6 min · bonus

The court wants the full story of methane and chlorine in UV light, step by step.

1. Name the mechanism. 2. Write the initiation step. 3. Write two propagation steps. 4. Write one termination step. 5. Why is a mixture of products formed?
Reveal
1 Free-radical substitution.
2 Cl2 → 2Cl• (homolytic fission, UV light).
3 Cl• + CH4 → •CH3 + HCl and •CH3 + Cl2 → CH3Cl + Cl•
4 •CH3 + Cl• → CH3Cl (or Cl• + Cl• → Cl2, or 2•CH3 → C2H6)
5 Further substitution gives CH2Cl2, CHCl3 and CCl4, and termination can make ethane.
DThe Addition Detective4 min · bonus

Alkenes have a double-bond secret that alkanes don't.

1. What happens when ethene meets bromine water? Write the equation.
2. Which product mainly forms from propene + HBr?
Reveal
1 Orange bromine water turns colourless: C2H4 + Br2 → C2H4Br2 (1,2-dibromoethane). This is the test for unsaturation.
2 2-bromopropane is the major product, because the more stable secondary carbocation forms. 1-bromopropane is minor.
